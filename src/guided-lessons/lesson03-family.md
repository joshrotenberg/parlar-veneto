# Lesson 3: Talking About Family

## Your Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can say whether you have siblings, identify close
family members, and say someone's age.

## Dialogue

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *Te ga fradèi?* | *Hai fratelli?* | Do you have siblings? |
| *Sì, go un fradel e na soreła.* | *Sì, ho un fratello e una sorella.* | Yes, I have a brother and a sister. |
| *Come se ciàma-lo?* | *Come si chiama?* | What's his name? |
| *El se ciama Marco. El ga vintidó ani.* | *Si chiama Marco. Ha ventidue anni.* | His name is Marco. He is 22. |
| *E to soreła?* | *E tua sorella?* | And your sister? |
| *La se ciama Sara. La ga vinti ani.* | *Si chiama Sara. Ha vent'anni.* | Her name is Sara. She is 20. |
| *To pare e to mare i sta qua?* | *Tuo padre e tua madre abitano qui?* | Do your father and mother live here? |
| *No, i sta a Pàdova.* | *No, abitano a Padova.* | No, they live in Padua. |

## Notice

### 1. *Gavér* Changes with the Person

| Person | Venetian | English |
|--------|----------|---------|
| I | *go* | I have |
| you | *te ga* | you have |
| he | *el ga* | he has |
| she | *la ga* | she has |
| they | *i ga* | they have |

The small words *te*, *el*, *la*, and *i* identify the subject. Do not translate
them word for word every time; learn each pattern as a unit.

### 2. Age Uses *Gavér*, as in Italian

- *Go trenta ani.* — I am 30.
- *El ga vintidó ani.* — He is 22.
- *Quanti ani te ga?* — How old are you?

### 3. Close Family Often Has No Article

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *me mare* | *mia madre* | my mother |
| *to pare* | *tuo padre* | your father |
| *so fradel* | *suo fratello* | his or her brother |

This resembles Italian, but the Venetian possessive does not change for gender:
*to pare* and *to mare* both use *to*.

## Family Words

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *pare* | *padre* | father |
| *mare* | *madre* | mother |
| *fradel* / *fradeo* | *fratello* | brother |
| *soreła* / *sorea* | *sorella* | sister |
| *nono* / *nona* | *nonno* / *nonna* | grandfather / grandmother |
| *fiol* / *fiola* | *figlio* / *figlia* | son / daughter |
| *moroso* / *morosa* | *fidanzato* / *fidanzata* | boyfriend / girlfriend |

## Practice

### A. Choose the Subject Clitic

Use **te**, **el**, **la**, or **i**.

1. Marco ___ ga vintidó ani.
2. Sara ___ se ciama Sara.
3. Ti ___ ga fradèi?
4. Me pare e me mare ___ sta a Verona.

### B. Translate into Venetian

1. I have a sister.
2. My father is called Marco.
3. How old are you?
4. They live in Padua.

### C. Describe Two People

Choose two real or imaginary people. For each person, say:

1. Their relationship to you
2. Their name
3. Their age or where they live

Models:

- *El xe me fradel. El se ciama Luca. El ga trenta ani.*
- *La xe me morosa. La se ciama Sara. La sta a Treviso.*

## Answer Key

**A:** 1. *el*; 2. *la*; 3. *te*; 4. *i*.

**B:** 1. *Go na soreła.* 2. *Me pare el se ciama Marco.* 3. *Quanti ani te ga?* 4. *I sta a Pàdova.*

## Regional Note

Family words vary even between nearby towns. You may hear *fradel*, *fradeo*, or
another local realization; *soreła* may sound like *sorea*. Family vocabulary is
often where a speaker's most local language survives. Copy the forms used by the
family or community you are speaking with.

---

*La fameja xe el primo posto dove che se inpara parlar.*

The family is the first place where one learns to speak.
