# Lesson 2: At the Bacaro

## Your Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can say that you are hungry or thirsty, order a
drink and a snack, and ask for the bill.

## Dialogue

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *Go sé. Ndemo in bacaro?* | *Ho sete. Andiamo in un bacaro?* | I'm thirsty. Shall we go to a bacaro? |
| *Va ben.* | *Va bene.* | All right. |
| *Mi ciapo un'ombra. E ti?* | *Io prendo un bicchiere di vino. E tu?* | I'll have a glass of wine. And you? |
| *Mi un spritz. Go anca fame.* | *Per me uno spritz. Ho anche fame.* | A spritz for me. I'm hungry too. |
| *Ciapemo dó spuncioti.* | *Prendiamo due spuntini.* | Let's have two snacks. |
| *Un'ombra, un spritz e dó spuncioti, par piaser.* | *Un bicchiere di vino, uno spritz e due spuntini, per favore.* | A wine, a spritz, and two snacks, please. |
| *Salute!* | *Salute!* | Cheers! |

## Notice

### 1. Venetian Uses *Gavér* for Physical States

This works much like Italian *avere*:

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *Go fame.* | *Ho fame.* | I'm hungry. |
| *Go sé.* | *Ho sete.* | I'm thirsty. |
| *Go fredo.* | *Ho freddo.* | I'm cold. |
| *Go caldo.* | *Ho caldo.* | I'm hot. |

The first-person form is *go*, not Italian *ho*. See
[Chapter 10](../part3-verbal/ch10-auxiliaries.md) for the full verb.

### 2. *Ciapar* Often Means “To Take” or “To Have”

- *Ciapo un'ombra.* — I'll have a glass of wine.
- *Ciapemo dó spuncioti.* — We'll have two snacks.

The endings are useful beyond this one verb:

- **-o**: I — *ciapo*
- **-emo**: we — *ciapemo*

### 3. *Mi un Spritz* Leaves Out an Obvious Verb

In conversation, speakers often omit words that everyone understands:

- *Mi un spritz.* — A spritz for me.
- *Mi un café.* — A coffee for me.

This is a useful phrase, not a new conjugation to memorize.

## Bacaro Vocabulary

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *un'ombra* | *un bicchiere di vino* | a small glass of wine |
| *un spritz* | *uno spritz* | a spritz |
| *un spuncioto* | *uno spuntino* | a snack |
| *dó* | *due* | two |
| *par piaser* | *per favore* | please |
| *el conto* | *il conto* | the bill; check |
| *salute!* | *salute!* | cheers! |

## Practice

### A. Choose *Son* or *Go*

1. ___ sé.
2. ___ de Treviso.
3. ___ fame.
4. ___ a casa.

### B. Build the Order

Put the pieces in a natural order:

1. *par piaser / dó spritz*
2. *un'ombra / mi / ciapo*
3. *el conto / par piaser*

### C. Order for Yourself

Say one complete order using this frame:

> *[drink] e [snack], par piaser.*

Then add *El conto, par piaser*.

## Answer Key

**A:** 1. *Go*; 2. *Son*; 3. *Go*; 4. *Son*.

**B:** 1. *Dó spritz, par piaser.* 2. *Mi ciapo un'ombra.* 3. *El conto, par piaser.*

## Regional Note

*Bacaro*, *ombra*, and *spuncioto* are especially associated with Venice and its
lagoon drinking culture. Elsewhere in the Veneto you may simply hear *bar*,
*osteria*, *vin*, or a different local word for the food. The grammar in the
dialogue remains useful everywhere.
