# Lesson 1: Meeting Someone

## Your Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can greet someone, give your name, say where you
are from, and explain that you do not understand Venetian yet.

## Dialogue

Read the Venetian aloud. The Italian column helps you recognize the structure;
the English column confirms the meaning.

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *Ciao! Come steto?* | *Ciao! Come stai?* | Hi! How are you? |
| *Stago ben, grassie. E ti?* | *Sto bene, grazie. E tu?* | I'm well, thanks. And you? |
| *Ben. Mi me ciamo Ana. Come te ciàmito?* | *Bene. Io mi chiamo Ana. Come ti chiami?* | Good. My name is Ana. What's your name? |
| *Mi me ciamo Marco.* | *Io mi chiamo Marco.* | My name is Marco. |
| *De dove sito?* | *Di dove sei?* | Where are you from? |
| *Son dei Stati Uniti. E ti?* | *Sono degli Stati Uniti. E tu?* | I'm from the United States. And you? |
| *Son de Pàdova. Te parli veneto?* | *Sono di Padova. Parli veneto?* | I'm from Padua. Do you speak Venetian? |
| *No, no lo capisso ancora.* | *No, non lo capisco ancora.* | No, I don't understand it yet. |

## Notice

### 1. *Son* Means “I Am”

Italian *sono* normally becomes Venetian *son*:

- *Son de Pàdova.* — I am from Padua.
- *Son de California.* — I am from California.

You can add emphatic *mi* when you want to contrast yourself with someone else:

- *Mi son de Pàdova. E ti?* — I am from Padua. And you?

### 2. A Second-Person Verb Needs *te*

Compare:

| Italian | Venetian | English |
|---------|----------|---------|
| *Tu parli.* | *Ti te parli.* | You speak. |
| *Come ti chiami?* | *Come te ciàmito?* | What's your name? |

The independent pronoun *ti* can be omitted, but the small subject clitic *te*
normally remains. In many questions it reappears as the ending *-to*:

- *steto?* — are you?
- *sito?* — are you?
- *ciàmito?* — are you called?

You will study the full system in
[Chapter 7](../part2-nominal/ch07-pronouns.md).

### 3. Put *No* Before the Verb Group

- *Capisso.* — I understand.
- *No capisso.* — I do not understand.
- *No lo capisso ancora.* — I do not understand it yet.

## Useful Pieces

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *ciao* | *ciao* | hi; bye |
| *ben* | *bene* | well; fine |
| *grassie* | *grazie* | thank you |
| *e ti?* | *e tu?* | and you? |
| *de dove?* | *di dove?* | from where? |
| *ancora* | *ancora* | still; yet |

## Practice

### A. Match the Meaning

1. *Come steto?*
2. *De dove sito?*
3. *No lo capisso ancora.*
4. *Mi me ciamo Luca.*

a. My name is Luca.
b. Where are you from?
c. How are you?
d. I do not understand it yet.

### B. Complete the Sentence

1. *___ de California.* — I am from California.
2. *Come te ___?* — What's your name?
3. *No lo ___ ancora.* — I do not understand it yet.
4. *Ti te ___ italian?* — Do you speak Italian?

Use: **son**, **ciàmito**, **capisso**, **parli**.

### C. Make It Yours

Say these four lines without reading the dialogue:

1. My name is ...
2. I am from ...
3. I speak a little Italian.
4. I do not understand Venetian yet.

A useful model for line 3 is *Parlo un poco de italian.*

## Answer Key

**A:** 1-c, 2-b, 3-d, 4-a.

**B:** 1. *Son*; 2. *ciàmito*; 3. *capisso*; 4. *parli*.

## Regional Note

You may hear different forms for “how are you?”, including *Come steto?* and
*Come xeła?* The goal is recognition, not choosing a winner. Use one form
consistently and notice what the people around you use.
