# Start Speaking: Your First Ten Minutes

This page gets you using Venetian before you study the grammar in detail. The
forms below follow the central terraferma variety used throughout this book.
You will hear other forms—and other spellings—from speakers elsewhere in the
Veneto. Treat that variation as part of the language, not as noise to eliminate.

## Four Sounds to Notice

You do not need perfect pronunciation to begin. Start with these four habits:

- **x** sounds like the *s* in English *rose*: *xe* /ze/ (is).
- **j** sounds like the *y* in English *yes*: *fijo* (son).
- Most Italian doubled consonants become single: Italian *tutto* becomes *tuto*.
- **ł** marks a historical *l* that may be weakened, vocalized, or absent,
  depending on the speaker. Listen before trying to force a single sound.

See [Chapter 1](../part1-phonology/ch01-sound-system.md) for the sound system and
[Chapter 3](../part1-phonology/ch03-orthography.md) for the spelling used here.

## A First Conversation

Read this once for meaning, then say each line aloud.

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *Ciao! Come steto?* | *Ciao! Come stai?* | Hi! How are you? |
| *Stago ben, grassie. E ti?* | *Sto bene, grazie. E tu?* | I'm well, thanks. And you? |
| *Ben. Mi son Marco. Come te ciàmito?* | *Bene. Io sono Marco. Tu come ti chiami?* | Good. I'm Marco. What's your name? |
| *Mi me ciamo Ana. De dove sito?* | *Io mi chiamo Ana. Di dove sei?* | My name is Ana. Where are you from? |
| *Son de Pàdova. E ti?* | *Sono di Padova. E tu?* | I'm from Padua. And you? |
| *Son de Visensa, ma desso stago qua.* | *Sono di Vicenza, ma adesso sto qui.* | I'm from Vicenza, but I live here now. |
| *Ah, ben! Ndemo?* | *Ah, bene! Andiamo?* | Ah, good! Shall we go? |
| *Volentiera. Ndemo!* | *Volentieri. Andiamo!* | Gladly. Let's go! |

Do not try to memorize every word. Notice the reusable pieces: *son* (I am),
*te* before a second-person verb, and the question ending *-to* in *steto?* and
*sito?*.

## Eight Building Blocks

These patterns let you make many useful sentences immediately.

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------|
| *Son...* | *Son de Treviso.* | I am / I'm from... |
| *Te si...* | *Te si pronto?* | You are / Are you...? |
| *Xe...* | *Xe bon.* | It is... |
| *Go...* | *Go fame.* | I have... / I'm... |
| *Ghe xe...* | *Ghe xe un problema.* | There is / There are... |
| *Me piase...* | *Me piase sto posto.* | I like... |
| *Vago...* | *Vago in casa.* | I'm going... |
| *No...* | *No capisso.* | I do not... |

The small word before many second- and third-person verbs is not optional:

- *Ti **te** parli veneto.* — You speak Venetian.
- ***El** xe de Pàdova.* — He is from Padua.
- *Ela **la** parla pian.* — She speaks slowly.

That subject-clitic system is one of the clearest differences between Venetian
and Italian. [Chapter 7](../part2-nominal/ch07-pronouns.md) explains it fully.

## When You Get Stuck

| Venetian | Italian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *No capisso.* | *Non capisco.* | I don't understand. |
| *Parla pian, par piaser.* | *Parla piano, per favore.* | Speak slowly, please. |
| *Cossa vol dir?* | *Cosa vuol dire?* | What does it mean? |
| *Come se dise in veneto?* | *Come si dice in veneto?* | How do you say it in Venetian? |
| *Pol-to repetir?* | *Puoi ripetere?* | Can you repeat? |
| *Grassie.* | *Grazie.* | Thank you. |

## A Five-Minute Practice

1. Read the conversation aloud twice.
2. Replace Marco, Ana, Pàdova, and Visensa with your own details.
3. Make one sentence each with *son*, *go*, *me piase*, and *no*.
4. Say the repair phrases above without looking.

You can now introduce yourself, ask a simple question, and keep a conversation
going when you miss a word. Continue with
[Lesson 1: Meeting Someone](../guided-lessons/lesson01-meeting.md), or jump to
[Greetings and Everyday Expressions](../part5-vocabulary/ch20-core-vocabulary.md#202-greetings-and-everyday-expressions).
